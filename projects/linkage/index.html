About

A series of worked examples for the mechanism unit in Technology. Each starts from the same question: with nothing but a turning disc, how do you make something move the way you want?

This page explains the basic four-bar linkage, using a wooden fish to strike as the exercise. Every example after it has its own page with a simulator you can adjust, a small challenge and a 1:1 worksheet.

四根桿、四個軸

四連桿是四根桿子用四個軸首尾相接成一圈。固定的那根叫固定桿；轉整圈的叫曲柄；來回擺的叫搖桿；夾在中間、把動作傳過去的叫連桿。

敲木魚裡，小和尚的身體是固定桿 DA（肚子 D 到肩膀 A），肚子上的圓盤是曲柄 CD，拿槌子的手臂是搖桿 AB，從圓盤接到手肘的是連桿 BC。桿長是兩個孔之間的距離，和零件的外形無關。

Four bars, four pivots

A four-bar linkage is four bars joined end to end by four pivots. The bar held still is the fixed bar; the one that turns all the way round is the crank; the one that swings back and forth is the rocker; the one in between, passing the motion along, is the coupler.

In the wooden fish, the little monk's body is the fixed bar DA (belly D to shoulder A), the disc on his belly is the crank CD, the arm holding the mallet is the rocker AB, and the bar from the disc to the elbow is the coupler BC. A bar's length is the distance between its two holes; the outline of the part does not matter.

為什麼轉得動

曲柄要轉一整圈，有兩個條件：最短的桿加最長的桿，不能比另外兩根加起來長；而且最短的那根要是曲柄。敲木魚是 2 + 5 ≤ 4 + 3.6，曲柄 2 cm 也最短，所以轉得動。

剛好相等叫做臨界：桿子會拉成一直線，紙卡做的會因為摩擦和孔位誤差卡住。超過了，曲柄只能來回擺。

Why it turns

The crank goes all the way round on two conditions: the shortest bar plus the longest must not be longer than the other two together, and the shortest must be the crank. For the fish that is 2 + 5 ≤ 4 + 3.6 with a 2 cm crank, so it turns.

When the two sides are equal the linkage is critical: the bars line up straight, and a card one jams on friction and small hole errors. Past that, the crank can only rock back and forth.

動手做

材料：紙卡（厚 1–2 mm）、圖釘 4 支、珍珠板、剪刀、口紅膠。

  1. 用外側的小和尚，或在內頁格子上畫自己的角色和要敲的東西，孔位不動，交給老師確認。
  2. 沿虛線裁下上方的保留帶留著，把零件頁貼到紙卡上，或描到紙卡上。
  3. 剪下小和尚、圓盤、連桿、手臂與木槌、木魚共 5 片，圖釘對準十字戳出孔。
  4. 小和尚平放在珍珠板上；圓盤疊在肚子 D、手臂疊在肩膀 A，各用一支圖釘釘穿到珍珠板。連桿接圓盤的 C 和手臂的 B。
  5. 木魚貼在小和尚前面的虛線上，順時鐘轉圓盤：舉槌慢、敲下快。

Make one

You need card 1–2 mm thick, four thumbtacks, foam board, scissors and a glue stick.

  1. Use the monk on the outside, or draw your own character and what it strikes on the grid inside. Leave the holes where they are and have the teacher check it.
  2. Cut off the top strip along the dashed line and keep it. Glue the parts page onto card, or trace it across.
  3. Cut out the five parts: monk, disc, coupler, arm with mallet, and fish. Push a thumbtack through each cross.
  4. Lay the monk on the foam board. Pin the disc at the belly D and the arm at the shoulder A right through to the board, and join the coupler from C on the disc to B on the arm.
  5. Glue the fish on the dashed outline and turn the disc clockwise: a slow lift, a quick strike.
